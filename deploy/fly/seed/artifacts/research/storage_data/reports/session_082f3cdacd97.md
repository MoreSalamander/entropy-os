## Executive Summary

Research indicates that a phase transition modulates the protective function of tardigrade disordered proteins during desiccation, while labile assembly induces biostasis in these extremotolerant organisms. Although *Viridiscus viridianus* survives high temperatures when hydrated without prior stress protection, other species exhibit differences in thermal tolerance depending on their hydration state and evolutionary history. Ecology explains anhydrobiotic performance across Tardigrada through processes such as osmobiosis and chemobiosis alongside complex adaptations to temperature extremes. The basic science behind these extremophiles can be applied to engineered versions tailored for biomanufacturing needs within synthetic biology, though outstanding questions remain regarding how extreme properties might be enhanced or transferred. Specifically, the study of CAHS D proteins in *Milnesium tardigradum* highlights ongoing efforts to understand the limits of life and potential applications in biotechnology. Ultimately, while significant progress has been made in understanding cryptobiosis, researchers continue to investigate whether these unique biological traits can be combined for new synthetic organisms.
