What is this — and what is it part of?

Veritas treats a language model as a proposal engine and a deterministic scaffold as the decision engine. The model is never trusted on its word. Its code only ships if it survives gates that check real properties — that a sort returns a sorted permutation of its input, that reversing a list twice gives back the original — facts that must hold for every input, not just the one example the model happened to pick.

The same architecture isn't limited to functions. The engine underneath — an artifact that has to earn trust, a gate that decides, a memory that learns from what it accepted and what it refused — also drives organizations that verify web pages (rendered and checked, not eyeballed), research (every claim cited to a source), and produced media (every stage traced to the last). This page exposes one of them, the code builder, and exposes it safely: your work runs isolated in a throwaway sandbox, and your account and usage are yours alone.

Veritas Dynamics is the capstone of the MoreSalamander studio — a study in building software organizations that are trustworthy by construction rather than by inspection. What you're holding is its smallest visible slice: proof that the loop works, in your hands, in a few seconds.
